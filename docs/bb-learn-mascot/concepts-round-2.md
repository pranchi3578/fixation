# BB Learn character: round 2, fourteen concepts from five angles

Sheet: `concepts_v2.png` (regenerate with `python3 concepts_v2.py`). Every concept
is drawn on the same grid, in black and 35% black, so they compare fairly. They are
all rough sketches; the question is *which idea*, not which drawing.

Round 1 went straight from "voice tutor" to "speech bubble". This round asks five
different questions first.

---

## Angle 1 · The name: *BrainBack* sounds like *backpack*, and like learning that *comes back*

**01 · Basta: the school bag that talks** (बस्ता, "school bag", in Hindi and Urdu)
- *Idea:* BrainBack → brain-pack → backpack. Every child carries one. The bag is
  where the lessons live, and the **flap is the mouth**: it opens when it talks.
- *Why it works:* it belongs to the child, not the institution. It's a warm
  object, and nobody reads it as "AI". It has great states: the flap talks, the zip
  closes when a topic is done, the pocket pulls out a pixel board to **show**.
- *Risk:* Dora the Explorer's talking Backpack is famous. Ours has to be square,
  pixel-built and faceless-until-it-speaks, so it doesn't read as a copy.

**02 · Wapas: a boomerang** (वापस, "back")
- *Idea:* what you learn comes back to you; you can ask again and again.
- *Risk:* at 16px it reads as a ▶ play/next arrow (see the favicon row). It's also
  hard to give a face. **Weak.**

## Angle 2 · What it does: talks, shows, fixes, waits, stops when you speak

**03 · Bubble** (round 1's pick). Direct and friendly, and the face reads at 16px.
Round 2 found its real weakness: *every* chat app has a bubble (iMessage, WhatsApp,
Messenger, Discord, a hundred support widgets). **Low ownability.**

**04 · Tarang: a voice wave with a face** (तरंग, "wave")
- *Idea:* it's voice-native, so the character *is* its voice. The bars move when it
  talks, go flat when the child speaks (**interrupt anytime**), and ripple while it
  thinks.
- *Risk:* voice waves are the stock image for every voice assistant. It animates
  best, but it is the least ownable when standing still.

**05 · Blink: the text cursor**
- *Idea:* the caret's blink *is* waiting. "It waits until he can say it himself."
- *Risk:* BB Learn is voice-first, and a caret says "type". Kids don't know what a
  caret is. **Better as the thinking/waiting state of another character.**

**06 · Unmute: the mic** (from the ad)
- *Idea:* ask anything, interrupt anytime, and the mic stays open.
- *Risk:* the mic is the *child's* tool, not the tutor. A mic with a face on a
  children's product also suggests "always recording", which is the wrong
  signal for parents and schools.

## Angle 3 · The brand system: one pixel of the grid, and compounding growth

**07 · Bit: one active cell of the brand grid wakes up**
- *Idea:* the purest BrainBack mark possible. It's one of the pulsing pixels from
  `patterns.md`, and nobody else owns this grid.
- *Risk:* it says nothing about teaching, and at 16px it's a dotted square. **Better
  as a BrainBack company-wide idea** (every product's character is an active cell)
  than as BB Learn's own.

**08 · Ankur: a sprout that grows** (अंकुर, "sprout")
- *Idea:* "gets better every week" and BrainBack's *compounding* value, made
  literal. **The mark itself evolves:** a leaf pixel for every milestone
  (languages, lessons taught).
- *Risk:* seeds and sprouts are generic ed-tech imagery, and it's messy at 16px.
  **The growth mechanic is the good part, not the sprout**; see "Ideas for any
  character" below.

## Angle 4 · India's classroom: the objects every Indian child already knows

**09 · Patti: the slate** (पट्टी / slate, सिलेट, സ്ലേറ്റ്, சிலேட்டு)
- *Idea:* before notebooks, every Indian child learns their first letters on a
  slate. It's the **ancestor of the tablet** they now learn on. The body is the
  black slate, the frame is 35% ink (the wood), and the face is drawn in chalk,
  so it's white pixels on black.
- *Why it's strong:*
  - **It shows.** Its face is a screen. It can wipe its own face and draw the
    lesson (a sum, a letter in any of 90 scripts, a diagram), then draw the face
    back. "It talks, it shows" in one object.
  - **It fixes.** It wipes the wrong line and redraws it gently, one stroke at a
    time.
  - **It's already the brand.** White pixels on a black board is exactly
    BrainBack's dark PixelBoard (`patterns.md` §1.3). Monochrome isn't a
    restriction here; it's the true colour of a slate.
  - **Nobody in ed-tech owns it,** and it speaks to parents (nostalgia) and to
    schools (a teacher's tool, not a toy).
- *Risk:* it could read as old-fashioned; the chalk-on-pixels look has to feel
  current. Under 24px the frame drops away and it becomes a black square with
  eyes, which is close to Bit. That's acceptable, even a nice rhyme.

**10 · Pulli: a kolam**
- *Idea:* BrainBack's pixel grid is a *pulli* dot grid. One unbroken line drawn
  around the dots makes the creature, the way a kolam or rangoli is drawn at the
  door each morning.
- *Risk:* it's beautiful as an animation (the line draws itself) but **illegible at
  16px**, and it reads as South Indian specifically. Keep it for patterns and
  loaders.

**11 · Akshar: a letter with eyes** (अक्षर, "letter")
- *Idea:* the first letter a child learns. Its eyes rest on the shirorekha (the
  headline stroke), and the body **morphs from script to script** (अ → அ → అ → অ → അ…)
  while the eyes stay the same: "every Indian language".
- *Risk:* the default glyph is political. A Devanagari-first mascot can read as
  Hindi-first. That can be solved by making the default state rotate, or be
  regional per deployment, but it needs a deliberate decision.

## Angle 5 · The ad's story: the doubt arrives, the tutor answers, the doubt flies

**12 · Doubtling**, **13 · Answered**, **14 · Duo** (round 1's A and C, plus the pair).
The Doubtling is a great *character*, but it's the problem, not the product.
The paper plane reads as a "send" icon (Telegram), and the duo turns to mush at
16px. **Keep these as the story world around whichever character wins.**

## Left out on purpose

| Idea | Why not |
|---|---|
| **Owl** | Duolingo owns it outright |
| **Robot** | Cold. It also feeds the "AI replaces teachers" fear, which schools and parents are the most wary of |
| **Brain** | Every AI company uses one. It's also squishy and gross at pixel scale, and too literal a reading of the name |
| **Parrot** (tota) | It talks, but a parrot *repeats*: rote learning is the opposite of the promise |
| **Lightbulb, graduation cap, book, rocket** | Generic ed-tech clip art |
| **A human teacher's face** | Likeness problems, and it implies a replacement for the teacher |
| **Anything orange / Clawd-shaped** | Too close to Claude's marks |

## Ideas for any character

These work with whichever character wins, so they aren't tied to one concept.

1. **The mark grows with you** (from Ankur). The character gains one pixel of
   detail at real milestones, like a new language shipped or a millionth lesson.
   It's BrainBack's "self-evolving" promise, shown in the logo itself.
   The rule: only add pixels, never redraw, so v1 is always inside vN.
2. **Waiting is a blink** (from Blink). The thinking/waiting state is a caret-like
   blink or the brand's `sin³` pixel pulse, never a spinner.
3. **The Doubtling world** (from the ad). Whatever the character is, doubts show up
   as little crumpled Doubtlings, and answering one folds it into a plane.
4. **Hello in every script** (from Akshar). The character writes or says its
   greeting in the child's script: नमस्ते, வணக்கம், നമസ്കാരം… That's how "90+
   languages" shows up without any text.

## Recommendation

**Shortlist: Patti (the slate) first, then Basta (the bag) and Bubble.**

Patti wins because it's the only concept that does all three product moves
*in its own body*. It talks with the chalk mouth, **shows** by using its face as the
screen, and fixes by wiping and redrawing. It's also native to BrainBack's
monochrome PixelBoard and to every Indian classroom, and no ed-tech brand owns it.
With the growth mechanic and the Doubtling world around it, the story becomes:
*a doubt lands on the slate, the slate talks it through, draws the answer, and the
doubt folds into a plane and flies off.*

Next step: take the top 2–3 to a proper master drawing, three versions each,
test them at 16px, on black and in motion, and pick one.
